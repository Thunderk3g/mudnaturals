# Nepali Handicraft / Artisan / Sustainable Commerce — Competitive Intelligence, Batch B

**Prepared for:** MUD Naturals (community-driven curated natural & handcrafted goods)
**Evidence discipline:** `[OBSERVED]` = read directly from the source · `[RECONSTRUCTED]` = assembled from cached snippets, directories, press, third-party listings · `[INFERENCE]` = reasoning from observed facts · `[UNKNOWN]` = could not verify.

**Access constraint (material):** corporate DNS filter (OpenDNS/Cisco Umbrella "shopping" category) blocked direct fetch of most `.com` retail domains — Kolpa, Mheecha, Sana Hastakala, Nepal Knotcraft, Local Project, Né Nepal, WSDO root, plus all Instagram/Facebook/LinkedIn and Google Play. Checkout/payment observations are therefore thin across the board and are marked `[UNKNOWN]` rather than guessed. Sites on `.org` / `.org.np` / agency domains fetched fine, so Drokpo, Aji's, SABAH and Bhairabi are the best-observed.

## 1. Seed Profiles

### 1.1 Saino — "Saino Marketplace"

**Disambiguation first, because the name is crowded.** `[OBSERVED]` At least four unrelated Nepali "Saino" entities exist: **Saino Marketplace** (the relevant one), **Saino | Matching Nepali Souls** (a dating app by Truenary), **Saino Tandoori & Sekuwa Corner** (restaurant), and **@saino_store** (an Instagram cap/hat seller). Do not brand-track on the bare word "Saino."

- **Positioning** `[OBSERVED]` A *producer-first* marketplace for "Made in Nepal & Make in Nepal." Explicit claim: it surfaces "the producer, the region and the story behind each item" rather than only the product. Supports artisans, women-led enterprises, cooperatives and small industries.
- **Products** `[RECONSTRUCTED]` Horizontal, not curated: handmade, organic, artisanal and locally manufactured goods across categories. Browsable by **district, province, community and producer profile** — geography-as-navigation is its distinctive IA. NPR price points `[UNKNOWN]`.
- **Ecommerce maturity** `[OBSERVED]` Unusually multi-channel for its size: consumer Android app (`com.upwear.userapp`), a separate **vendor app** (`com.upwear.seller_app`) for sellers to list products, manage orders and track payments; a web storefront at `sainomarketplace.com.np` (did not resolve — ENOTFOUND, possibly intermittent or geo/DNS-dependent); **three Daraz shopfronts** (`saino-craft`, `saino-store`, `jggvkqg2`); Facebook `@sainonp`; X `@SAINOMP`. App listing appears updated ~Feb 2026.
- **Storytelling** `[OBSERVED]` Producer profiles with "background, story and product origin" are a first-class product feature, not an About page. Strongest structural storytelling of any seed.
- **Impact** `[OBSERVED]` Claims "every purchase contributes to local income generation and community-level growth" and "verified producers." `[UNKNOWN]` No verification methodology, no certification, no impact numbers found.
- **Brand** `[INFERENCE]` Marketplace/utility perception rather than premium. Presence on Daraz alongside commodity goods pulls perceived price positioning down.
- **Model** `[RECONSTRUCTED]` Two-sided marketplace with seller self-service — asset-light, commission-based `[INFERENCE]`, scale-dependent.

### 1.2 Eco Dosti

**Weakest-evidence seed — treat as unconfirmed.** `[OBSERVED]` The only Nepal-linked "Eco Dosti" found is Instagram **@eco.dosti**, described in indexed metadata as a *student-led NGO aiming to create a better environment*, ~1,849 followers / 284 posts. `[OBSERVED]` `ecodosti.com` is a **different, Indian** business — Honesty Trading Co., Mumbai, est. 1984, wholesale handmade/gift-wrap/recycled paper. `[OBSERVED]` Repeated searches surface **Eco Sathi Nepal** instead — a genuinely operating zero-waste lifestyle brand (Dr. Manu Karki, dentist-founder, ideated 2018, launched 2019 on a Rs 19,000 loan from her mother; bamboo toothbrushes/combs/brushes, bottles, tote bags, cutlery, menstrual cups; EV-based delivery logistics; `ecosathinepal.com`).

- **Products / ecommerce / payments / impact evidence:** `[UNKNOWN]` for Eco Dosti.
- **`[INFERENCE]`** Either the seed name is a transcription of "Eco Sathi," or Eco Dosti is a pre-commercial student initiative with no meaningful retail footprint. **Recommendation: replace Eco Dosti with Eco Sathi Nepal in the competitive set unless the client can supply a URL.**

### 1.3 Drokpo — `drokpo.org.np` (note: `.org.np`, not `.com`)

- **Positioning** `[OBSERVED]` NGO-first, commerce-second. Registered nonprofit (est. 2004) serving Tibetan and Himalayan communities, especially women. "Drokpo" = *friend* in Tibetan. Commerce runs as a named sub-brand, **Drokpo Mercantile**, launched for the org's 20th anniversary as "a movement that unites mindful consumers, local artisans, and our organization's mission."
- **Products** `[OBSERVED]` 13 categories, narrow and curated: prayer flags/lungta, biodegradable khatak, notebooks, **eco-pads (reusable menstrual pads)**, cotton totes, greeting cards, hemp shawls, incense, natural wooden bracelets, personal care, stationery, stickers, felted wool dolls. Materials named: organic cotton with plant dyes, cotton flannel, hemp, and **recycled non-biodegradable waste sourced from the Everest region** (incense/candle holders).
- **Pricing** `[OBSERVED]` Mixed-currency and inconsistent — small prayer flag $10, bookmark $1, tote $15, but Bodhi incense listed at **Rs. 350** (sold out). Low price points overall; not a premium ladder.
- **Ecommerce UX** `[OBSERVED]` WordPress-style category grid → "View Products." Header promises "Handmade in Nepal, Delivered Worldwide, **Verified Checkout**." Nav: About / Programs / Impact / Shop / Resources. `[UNKNOWN]` No payment methods disclosed on any reachable page; several SKUs sold out. `[OBSERVED]` Offline channels are real: Le Sherpa and Utpala farmers markets, community events, plus Instagram DM ordering.
- **Storytelling** `[OBSERVED]` Strong *cause* storytelling (women's health, street dogs, culture preservation, waste collection, education) but the **maker** is largely absent — no named artisans on product pages.
- **Impact** `[OBSERVED]` Member of **1% for the Planet**; Social Welfare Council reg. 44896; PAN 604367217; 1% of proceeds ringfenced for environmental initiatives; commerce revenue explicitly funds programmes. The most *verifiable* impact governance in the seed set.
- **Model** `[OBSERVED]` Nonprofit social enterprise; product revenue cross-subsidises programmes.

### 1.4 Bhairabi Handicraft

- **Positioning** `[OBSERVED]` "Handmade. Plastic-Free. Women-Led." A **B2B felt manufacturer and exporter**, not a consumer brand. Named for the goddess of courage; pitch is "change the way the world sources felt."
- **Products** `[OBSERVED]` 11 categories, all felt: shoes, felt balls, animal & pet products, finger puppets, keyrings, tea coasters, felt vegetables & fruits, bags & purses, home décor, Christmas/seasonal, and **custom & private label**. 100% natural sheep wool from Nepal's highlands, zero plastic packaging, no machine shortcuts.
- **Pricing** `[OBSERVED]` No public prices. Wholesale/retail quoted on request with volume tiers. `[UNKNOWN]` NPR retail points.
- **Ecommerce UX** `[OBSERVED]` Catalogue + enquiry, not a storefront. Payment rails listed are **card-only — Visa, Mastercard, Amex, Discover, Diners Club** (built for foreign buyers; no eSewa/Khalti/COD). Shipping via DHL/FedEx, 5–14 days standard, 2–4 weeks bulk. Buyer segments named: boutique retailers, wholesale importers, schools, gift brands, brand developers. `[OBSERVED]` Site served from `icebergtechnology.com.np/bhairabi/` — an agency path, suggesting a recent or vendor-hosted build `[INFERENCE]`.
- **Storytelling** `[OBSERVED]` Emotive and well-written, but at the *category* level: "When you order from Bhairabi, you are not just filling a shelf. You are funding a woman's independence." No named artisans, no village geography.
- **Impact** `[OBSERVED]` Claims 200+ small businesses served, 80,000+ orders, buyers in 20+ countries, "full batch traceability." `[OBSERVED]` **No third-party certification visible** — not WFTO, not Fair Trade Group Nepal. Claims are self-asserted.
- **Brand** `[INFERENCE]` Premium-*sounding* copy, mid-market felt-commodity category. Felt is Nepal's most crowded export craft, so differentiation rests entirely on the plastic-free + women-led narrative.

### 1.5 WSDO — Women's Skills Development Organization, Pokhara

- **Positioning** `[OBSERVED]` The institutional benchmark. Founded **1975** by rural craftswomen; momentum from entrepreneur **Ramkali Khadka** (joined 1977); formally registered 1994. Non-profit income-generation for economically disadvantaged, disabled, widowed, divorced, single and outcaste women.
- **Products** `[OBSERVED]` Broad, utility-led, low-priced: travel bags & backpacks **Rs 1,000–3,000**; cases, purses & wallets **Rs 150–3,000**; side bags **Rs 475–1,600**; shoulder bags **Rs 900–2,500**; homeware **Rs 200–3,500**; plus clothing, toys, footwear. Breadth over curation; traditional rather than contemporary.
- **Materials/process** `[OBSERVED]` Hand-woven **Dhaka** on backstrap looms; Indian cotton blended with **allo** (Himalayan nettle); skeins naturally dyed with tea, persimmon, pomegranate and Nepalese hops.
- **Ecommerce UX** `[OBSERVED]` A browsable catalogue under `/our-beautiful-items/products/` by category, plus a **"How to Order"** page, plus a PDF catalogue dating to 2017. `[OBSERVED]` Prices carry the caveat that they "vary often… get in touch for an up-to-date quote" — `[INFERENCE]` an **enquiry-to-invoice flow, not a live cart**. `[UNKNOWN]` Payment methods; site returned 403 to fetches.
- **Storytelling** `[OBSERVED]` Deep on organisational history and beneficiary category; thinner on individual makers on-site (richest maker narrative sits on *third-party* pages — The Textile Atlas, MUNIMUNI, Ganapati Crafts).
- **Impact** `[OBSERVED]` Strongest verified credentials of any seed: **founding member of Fair Trade Group Nepal (1996)**, **WFTO member since 2003**, 500+ female artisans, many living with disability, free vocational training then guaranteed employment. Export markets: Japan, Korea, Taiwan.
- **Brand** `[INFERENCE]` Trusted, worthy, dated. Fair-trade-institutional aesthetic, not contemporary-design aesthetic. Its impact is real and its brand under-monetises it.
- **Model** `[OBSERVED]` Non-profit; wholesale/export-led with a domestic retail tail.

## 2. Discovered Competitors (not on the seed list)

**Timro Concept Store** — `[OBSERVED]` Founded April 2017, Jhamsikhel, by Yogen Tamrakar and Dipendra Lama (started selling lokta notebooks at art markets). *Shop-in-shop*: founders share rent with 18–20+ partner brands (Sherpani Creations, Ekadesma, Studio Sarcastic, Hatti Hatti, Bhav, Resa Living, Tissah, Allare), each with a social-impact story; in-house café. Founders' insight directly relevant to MUD: *the good Nepali product gets exported, the domestic shelf gets the seconds.* `[UNKNOWN]` online checkout. **Relevance: this is MUD's curation model, executed physically.**

**The Local Project Nepal** — `[OBSERVED]` Patan/Lalitpur, ~20 local brands (Mheecha bags, Bora Studio apparel, Studio Sarcastic stationery, Allare games, Amuse books), 100% made-in-Nepal utility + artisan goods, `localprojectnepal.com`. Reviews consistently cite aesthetics and fair-trade framing. **Relevance: closest direct analogue to a curated MUD storefront, strongest brand aesthetic in the retail-hub tier.**

**Kolpa** — `[OBSERVED]` Social enterprise by Rabi C. Malla (Bhaktapur-named, after the lost settlement Kolpakot), working through family/community cottage industries and co-ops with profit-sharing. Materials named explicitly: Himalayan wild nettle *allo*, hemp *ganja*, corn husk *khosta*, typha *pater*, lokta, vegetable-tanned leather. `kolpaworld.com` prices in **USD**: card wallets $12.50–15, nettle market bags $30–45, lokta printer paper $18, nettle/cotton rolltop rucksack **$220**; free shipping over $100. **Relevance: best-executed "natural material + community maker" premium positioning in Nepal, priced for export, not Kathmandu.**

**Aji's** — `[OBSERVED]` `ajisproducts.org`. Founded Jan 2018 by sisters Lorina and Irina Sthapit with Pursarth Tuladhar, inspired by their grandmother Champa Devi Tuladhar. *Aji* = grandmother (Newari). ~30 elderly makers, ages **58–86**, working from home; children/grandchildren involved deliberately to strengthen family bonds. Range: hand-knitted socks, macramé plant hangers, Dhaka goods, papier-mâché, organic face scrub, silver jewellery, baby bhoto sets. Split channels: **Etsy for USA/international, own site for Kathmandu**; Patreon for patrons; two physical stores. BBC/Yahoo/Kathmandu Post coverage. **Relevance: sharpest maker-identity story in Nepal — a single, ownable human insight. This is the storytelling bar.**

**Mheecha** — `[OBSERVED]` Founded 2016 by Anish Bajracharya, Pratik Shakya, Nishan Bajracharya. *Mheecha* = pouch (Newa). Designed and manufactured wholly in Nepal; minimal, durable, contemporary. **NPR pricing observed:** Boulder Sack from Rs 1,550, Alley Backpack from Rs 2,350, Crony Sleeve Rs 2,950, backpacks broadly Rs 2,650–3,750. Sells own site + Daraz + third-party retailers + stocked by Local Project. **Relevance: proof that a contemporary Nepali brand can hold NPR 2–4k price points domestically without fair-trade framing.**

**Kaligarh** — `[OBSERVED]` *Kaligarh* = artisan. Contemporary jewellery by Jyoti Upadhyay, handmade by small-scale Himalayan artisans, packed in recycled-paper boxes; post-2015-earthquake housing/employment commitment. `[OBSERVED]` Retails through **UK channels** (La Maison Couture, Chicmi, Thought Socks). **Relevance: the export-boutique route — near-zero domestic ecommerce, high brand polish.**

**SABAH Nepal** — `[OBSERVED]` SAARC Business Association of Home-Based Workers, funded via the SAARC Development Fund. **3,000+ women members**; 272+ trainings reaching 3,785+ women; claims 15–25% reduction in intermediary margins. Infrastructure: **8 community centres, 6 retail outlets in Kathmandu Valley, 3 community cafés**, Trade Facilitation Centres, plus a weekly **Friday Market at Kusunti**. Products: apparel, fashion accessories, home decoration, knitwear; embroidery, crochet, macramé, vegetable dyes, Dhaka and allo-cotton handwoven fabric; philosophy of "beauty of imperfection." `[OBSERVED]` **No online shop found** — the biggest supply base with the weakest digital layer. **Relevance: a potential supply partner more than a competitor.**

**Nepal Knotcraft Centre** — `[OBSERVED]` Founded 1984 by Shyam Badan Shrestha; began with three women doing macramé. Now: 2,000+ women trained across **24 districts**; a Lumbini-area collective of 50+ weavers reviving sacred geometric patterns and natural dyes. Products: Terai **sikki (elephant grass) and kans baskets** — *dhakiya* (transport), *feruwa* (storage), *deluwa* (ceremonial, mother-to-daughter bridal gift) — plus bamboo baskets, cornhusk dolls, Dhaka textiles. A floral *dhakki* basket fetches **USD 120–130**. Sells through **Ten Thousand Villages (retail + wholesale)** and Etsy. **Relevance: the single closest product-category match to MUD's baskets, with genuine anthropological depth and named basket typologies MUD could license as vocabulary.**

**Dinadi** — `[OBSERVED]` Launched April 2016 (Preston & Mirjam, Kathmandu living room → Kickstarter 200%+ over goal). *Dinadi* = dawn. Merino knitwear, home-based knitters. Wage transparency is exceptional: **at least 2× competitor pay**, 28.33% of salary to retirement (10% employee + 18.33% employer), medical + accident insurance, emergency medical fund, **+25% of salary toward children's schooling**, full-time social worker doing home visits. **Relevance: the gold standard for evidenced impact claims — MUD will be benchmarked against this.**

**Purnaa** — `[OBSERVED]` **WFTO-guaranteed** ethical cut-and-sew manufacturer (knit tops, handbags, hats, custom printed headwear). Publishes its living-wage calculation: **Rs 43,108/month for a family of four**, computed using WFTO tools and agreed with employees. **Relevance: the manufacturing partner option for own-label MUD product.**

**Wild Earth** — `[OBSERVED]` `wildearthnepal.com`. ~30 handmade herbal soap varieties, cold-process, cured 1+ month, all-vegetable base with Himalayan herbal infusions and essential oils; yak-milk/red-clay/honey bar is the bestseller. Exports to US, Australia, Europe, Canada, Japan, Brazil; **passed Japanese Ministry of Health inspection for Tokyo sale**. `[OBSERVED]` Website is a legacy PHP catalogue, not a modern storefront. **Relevance: the natural-bodycare adjacency MUD would enter; strong product credibility, weak digital.**

**Marina Vaptzarova** — `[OBSERVED]` Nepal-based since 1993, led by French designer Marina Shrestha (in Nepal since 1981). Sustainable-luxury lokta journals, guestbooks, sketchbooks and interiors with hand-carved brass/silver; proprietary **"Daphley" vegetal leather** made from lokta fibre. Hospitality and B2B/showroom-led. **Relevance: the ceiling of what Nepali craft can charge — proof that material innovation, not heritage alone, unlocks luxury pricing.**

**Né Nepal** (`nenepal.shop`) — `[RECONSTRUCTED]` Design studio making everyday products entirely handmade in Nepal; "designs derived from local culture that fit effortlessly into modern lifestyle," minimal and functional, traditional methods. `[UNKNOWN]` prices, checkout. **Relevance: the most directly competitive *contemporary-design* positioning found.**

**Swodeshi** (`swodeshi.com`) — `[RECONSTRUCTED]` Made-in-Nepal marketplace, retail + wholesale, ships worldwide, **COD across Nepal** `[OBSERVED]`; handicrafts, cultural wear, home decor, kitchen items, wishlists/lists features. Traditional-leaning assortment (statues, thangka, khukuri, woodcraft). **Relevance: Saino's nearest rival in the horizontal marketplace lane.**

**Eco Sathi Nepal** — see §1.2. Women-owned zero-waste lifestyle brand, EV logistics, local sourcing.

**Secondary cluster (verified as operating, profiled only in brief)** `[RECONSTRUCTED]`: **Sana Hastakala** (1989, WFTO-guaranteed, **1,200+ producers**, ceramics/felt/textiles — the largest fair-trade retail brand); **HattiHatti** (upcycled saree accessories + weekly education for makers); **Kokroma** (baby clothing woven by prisoners); **Local Women's Handicrafts** (upcycled saree rugs/yoga mats); **Dhaka Weaves** (`dhakaweaves.org`, woman-run since 1991, silk/cotton/pashmina Dhaka scarves); **Sumdima Weaves** and **Dochaa** (contemporary Dhaka); **Jamarko** (recycled paper); **Tatwa** (upcycled candle holders, Bhaktapur); **Pia** (hand-hammered copper, Patan Durbar Square); **Cotton Mill Nepal** (khasto bedding); **Lakhey** (slow-fashion basics); **The Shampoo Bar Kathmandu**; **Dharti Mata** (reusable pads); **Juju Wears** (2013, printed tees; sibling brand **Karuna Natural Wears** in hemp/bamboo/nettle); **Himalayan Bio Trade** (community-forest-owned essential oils/allo).

## 4. Closest to MUD's Intended Territory

MUD's stated territory — *curated, community-made, natural/handcrafted homeware, baskets, natural lifestyle* — collides most directly with:

1. **The Local Project Nepal** and **Timro Concept Store.** `[INFERENCE]` Same job-to-be-done: curate many small Nepali makers under one trusted brand, contemporary aesthetic, Kathmandu-valley base. They own the physical-retail expression of exactly MUD's proposition and already hold the good brands. Their weakness is digital — neither has an observable modern online checkout — which is where MUD can take ground rather than fight.

2. **Nepal Knotcraft Centre.** `[INFERENCE]` The only player whose *core category is baskets*, with 40 years of supply depth across 24 districts and a genuine craft vocabulary (dhakiya/feruwa/deluwa, sikki/kans). It sells almost entirely through foreign fair-trade channels, so it competes with MUD on **supply access**, not on the shelf. Treat as partner-or-competitor depending on whether MUD sources or substitutes.

3. **Kolpa.** `[INFERENCE]` Nearest match on *positioning language* — community cottage industries, profit-sharing, named indigenous materials, contemporary product. It has already claimed the "natural Himalayan fibre, beautifully designed" territory and prices in USD, meaning it has ceded the domestic Nepali price tier that MUD could own.

4. **Saino Marketplace.** `[INFERENCE]` Competes for the same *narrative* — producer-first, geography-visible, community income — but as a horizontal marketplace. Curation is MUD's counter-move: Saino's breadth is its brand ceiling.

5. **Aji's** and **Drokpo** are adjacent rather than head-on (elder-maker niche; ritual/eco-pad niche) but both set the **storytelling and impact-governance bar** MUD's copy will be measured against. Drokpo's 1% for the Planet membership and SWC registration are cheap, concrete credibility signals MUD could match early.

`[INFERENCE]` **The open gap:** nobody in this set combines (a) contemporary curated homeware/baskets, (b) a modern mobile-first Nepali checkout with eSewa/Khalti/COD, and (c) named-maker, named-village provenance at product level. Mheecha proves the domestic price tier works; Aji's proves the maker story works; Local Project proves the curation works — but no single operator has all three online.

## 5. Open Questions

1. **Eco Dosti** — is this Eco Sathi Nepal, a private/unindexed venture, or a pre-revenue student initiative? Needs a URL or handle from the client before any further work.
2. **Payment rails** — checkout unobservable on Nepali retail sites due to DNS filter. Whether Kolpa, Mheecha, Local Project, Né Nepal and Saino actually offer **eSewa / Khalti / IME Pay / COD** is `[UNKNOWN]` and is the highest-value next check; run from an unfiltered network.
3. **Saino's real scale** — app install counts, ratings, live vendors, category depth, whether `sainomarketplace.com.np` is live.
4. **WSDO's actual checkout** — catalogue-with-quote or real cart? Affects whether it is a domestic ecommerce competitor at all.
5. **Bhairabi's certification status** — is the absence of WFTO/FTG Nepal membership a gap or simply undisclosed? Cross-check the Fair Trade Group Nepal member directory.
6. **NPR price ladders** for the curated-retail tier (Timro, Local Project, Né, Kolpa domestic) — the single missing input for MUD's own pricing architecture.
7. **Supply overlap risk** — SABAH Nepal (3,000+ home-based workers) and Nepal Knotcraft (24 districts) are the two largest artisan pools with the weakest brands. Whether MUD sources from them or competes with them is a strategic fork, not a research finding.
