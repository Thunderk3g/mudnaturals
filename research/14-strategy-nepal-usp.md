## 1. Competitor Gap Analysis

### What competitors already do extremely well
- **Supply relationships at scale** [E]: Mahaguthi 1,100+ makers, Sana Hastakala 1,200+, ACP 500–1,000+ across 8–12 districts, SABAH 3,000+, Knotcraft 2,000+ trained across 24 districts. Forty years of producer access. MUD cannot out-supply this cohort and should not try.
- **Third-party impact verification** [E]: WFTO Guaranteed status (Mahaguthi, Sana Hastakala, ACP, Purnaa), and quantified claims — Mahaguthi's "68% of sales revenue returns to artisans," Dinadi's full wage/benefits disclosure, Purnaa's published living-wage figure (Rs 43,108/month). The evidence bar for impact claims in Nepal is already high.
- **Physical retail curation** [E]: Timro Concept Store and Local Project Nepal have proven that a curated, contemporary, multi-maker Nepali store works commercially. Dhukuti runs a 3,000 sq ft flagship on a >$1M turnover.
- **Contemporary product at domestic price points** [E]: Mheecha holds Rs 1,550–3,750 domestically with no fair-trade framing. Kolpa executes named-indigenous-material product design ($12.50–$220, export-priced).
- **Marketplace infrastructure** [E]: Saino (consumer + vendor apps + Daraz), Himali Green (seller apps, 3% capped commission, geographic store directory), Swodeshi (eSewa + Khalti + COD nationwide).

### Table stakes (claimed by nearly everyone; zero differentiation value)
1. "Made in Nepal" / authenticity claims [E — universal]
2. "Supporting artisans / empowering women" copy [E — universal, from Bhairabi to Saino]
3. Natural-material vocabulary (allo, hemp, lokta) at the About-page level [E]
4. WhatsApp/Instagram DM as an order channel [E — Sana Hastakala's entire transaction, Drokpo, most small brands]
5. For the local buyer: **eSewa/Khalti/COD** — Swodeshi and the marketplaces have it; any serious domestic player must [E/SI]
6. Physical market presence (Le Sherpa, Utpala, Friday Market) for discovery [E]

### Claimed but not demonstrated
- **Traceability**: Bhairabi claims "full batch traceability" with zero visible mechanism; Saino claims "verified producers" with no methodology; Himali Green's badges are self-declared uploads [E]. *The word is everywhere; the artifact exists nowhere.*
- **"The story behind each item"**: universally claimed, delivered only at category level. No competitor links a specific product to a specific maker on the PDP [E — verified absence across both batches].
- **Contemporary design**: ACP claims "blending traditional craft with contemporary design" but merchandises as a "treasure trove" of 15 categories including Christmas/Easter export lines [E].

### Where consumers work too hard
- **Prices**: quote-only at WSDO, Sana Hastakala, ACP, Bhairabi. The premium domestic buyer cannot find out what anything costs without a WhatsApp conversation [E].
- **Payment**: methods undiscoverable pre-checkout on most sites [E].
- **Availability**: sold-out SKUs (Drokpo), empty stores (Dhukuti Online, HamroCraft's 404s), dead shops (mahaguthi.org "launching soon") [E].
- **Trust verification**: Mahaguthi's broken Social Impact link and boilerplate WooCommerce refund policy actively undermine a genuine 40-year impact record [E].

### Where the experience feels dated
Fair-trade cohort sites are WooCommerce-institutional (Mahaguthi), brochureware (ACP), or enquiry-catalogues (WSDO, Sana Hastakala). Wild Earth runs a legacy PHP catalogue. Best-in-class digital is a Shopify default theme (Kolpa) or Squarespace + Etsy (Aji's) [E]. **No Nepali craft brand has an international-grade storefront.**

### Where storytelling and commerce are disconnected
Mahaguthi: 7 named artisan profiles siloed from PDPs. Drokpo: rich cause storytelling, absent makers. SABAH: 3,000 members, no online shop at all. Knotcraft: deepest craft vocabulary in Nepal (dhakiya/feruwa/deluwa), sold anonymously through Ten Thousand Villages' brand [E].

### The opportunity (convergent finding, all three agents)
**Nobody combines: (a) curated contemporary homeware/baskets, (b) modern mobile-first Nepali checkout (eSewa/Khalti/COD), (c) named-maker, named-village provenance at product level.** Mheecha proves the price tier. Aji's proves the maker story. Timro/Local Project prove the curation. Dhukuti proves the turnover. No single operator has all of it online. This gap is **execution-shaped, not demand-shaped** — the pieces are individually proven.

Scoring rationale for the deciders:
- **B (provenance)** scores 5 on distinctiveness/defensibility because the gap analysis shows the *claim* is universal and the *artifact* nonexistent — and because provenance as a **data model** (maker → community → material → craft → product, rendered on every PDP) is structural: a competitor must rebuild their catalogue architecture and their supplier data collection to copy it, not just rewrite copy [SI]. It scores 5 on ecommerce compatibility because provenance is spec-shaped content that lives inside the buy decision (the Baymard "materials & manufacturing" story types users actually seek — see `02-artisan-marketplace-ux.md`).
- **A (contemporary craft house)** is desirable but weakly defensible — ACP already claims the words, Kolpa/Né occupy the aesthetic, and "contemporary" is a style, not a structure [E/SI].
- **C (living archive)** has the best storytelling and the worst commerce — it is Gaatha, and Gaatha's three-domain split is the documented failure mode [E].
- **D (impact-transparent)** fails on desirability: the impact-storytelling research (`04-impact-storytelling.md`) shows impact converts as substantiation, not as a lead proposition; and Dinadi/Mahaguthi already hold the evidence high ground [E].
- **F (gifting house)** is the strongest *revenue* insight (Giftmandu sells chocolate to banks; craft-grade corporate gifting is open [E]) but too narrow as an identity — it is a channel, not a position.

### Top 3
1. **B — Objects with origins** (46/50)
2. **F — Nepal's craft gifting house** (37/50)
3. **A — Contemporary Nepali craft house** (34/50)

## 4. Recommended USP

> **MUD Naturals is Nepal's curated craft house where every object carries its origin.**
> Position: **"Objects with origins"** — provenance-first contemporary craft commerce. Candidate A supplies the aesthetic register; Candidate F supplies the launch revenue engine (corporate + festival gifting); Candidate B is the identity and the architecture.

**Why it is differentiated.** Every Nepali competitor claims story; none delivers it at product level [E]. The fair-trade cohort has real provenance data and 2014-era digital; the contemporary cohort has design and no provenance structure; the marketplaces have breadth and no curation. Provenance-as-architecture sits at the intersection none of them can reach without becoming a different company.

**Why customers care.** The handmade effect is worth ~17% WTP when the making is legible (Fuchs et al., JM 2015). Baymard's DTC research shows users actively seek materials/manufacturing content and dismiss products without it. For the Nepali premium buyer, provenance converts a Rs 4,500 basket from "expensive dhakiya" into a named object from a named place — the price justification Thamel's haggling economy can never offer. For diaspora buyers, origin *is* the product.

**Proof MUD needs (in order of urgency).**
1. Named makers/communities with documented consent and a published consent policy (no competitor publishes one — cheap first-mover trust [E]).
2. A wage/price methodology page (benchmark: Mahaguthi's 68%, Purnaa's Rs 43,108 living wage — MUD will be measured against these [E]).
3. Product photography of making (hands/process/place) — the visual proof layer.
4. The provenance data itself: maker, community, district, material, technique, hours, batch — captured at intake for every SKU.

**What competitors would struggle to replicate.**
- Fair-trade incumbents: governance and catalogue breadth prevent per-SKU curation and rapid digital rebuilds; their institutional aesthetic is 40 years deep [SI].
- Contemporary brands (Kolpa, Mheecha, Né): single-founder capacity; no multi-maker supply network; copying provenance-at-scale requires the community relationships MUD's NGO partnership provides [SI].
- Marketplaces (Saino, Himali Green, Swodeshi): curation contradicts their business model — their revenue is breadth [SI].

**Effect on assortment.** Every SKU must clear two bars: contemporary usefulness (Home & Table discipline — baskets, storage, kitchen/dining, natural lifestyle at Rs 1,500–8,000) and documentable origin. If provenance can't be captured, the product doesn't launch. Small catalogue is a feature of the position, not a limitation.

**Effect on the website.** Provenance becomes the signature PDP module (see §7); Makers earn primary nav; craft vocabulary (sikki, kans, allo, lokta, dhakiya, feruwa, deluwa) becomes taxonomy; the impact page becomes a methodology archive, not a plea.

**Effect on storytelling.** Register = spec-sheet + maker's note (per `04-impact-storytelling.md` playbook): verbs of making, named people with consent, hours and materials, no charity vocabulary. The craft-vocabulary layer (basket typologies, material names in Nepali) is unique, credible, and un-copyable quickly.

**Effect on CRM/data structures.** Provenance is first-class schema, not CMS content: `makers`, `communities`, `materials`, `craft_techniques`, `products.maker_id`, per-batch attribution option, maker earnings ledger, consent records (name/photo/quote permissions with dates), impact rollups as materialized views. The admin needs a provenance-capture workflow at product intake — this is the single biggest delta from a stock ecommerce CRM.

## 5. Moats — real vs ordinary features

| Candidate moat | Verdict | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Community relationships** (long-term, via NGO partner) | **Real moat — the deepest one** | Supply access took incumbents 40 years [E]. MUD's NGO partnership is the only shortcut; everything else builds on it. |
| **Provenance data model** | **Real moat (compounding)** | Structural to copy; grows richer with every SKU/season; feeds SEO, storytelling, impact reporting simultaneously [SI]. |
| **Curation** | Real but soft | Taste is defensible only while consistently exercised; protects margin more than position [SI]. |
| **Brand** (contemporary Nepali lifestyle identity) | Real over time | Vacant position + consistent execution = ownable; but brand is the *output* of the other moats, not standalone [SI]. |
| **Content/knowledge base** (materials, crafts, places) | Real accumulator, slow | Knotcraft's typology depth shows the value; only a moat if tied to commerce (Gaatha shows the failure mode) [E]. |
| **Impact data** (measurable value creation) | Ordinary feature → moat only if audited | Self-declared numbers are table stakes; WFTO-grade or third-party-verified per-maker data would be a moat but is expensive [E/SI]. |
| **Product development** (technique → contemporary product) | Ordinary feature at launch | Constrained by the national design-capability gap [E]; becomes a moat only with an in-house design function (phase 2+). |
| **Storytelling** | Ordinary feature | Copyable in months; only the underlying data and relationships are not [SI]. |

**Moat stack [R]:** community relationships → provenance data model → curation discipline → brand. Invest in that order.

## 6. Business Model: Marketplace vs Curated Brand vs Hybrid

| Criterion | A. Open marketplace | B. Curated brand | C. Hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer experience | Breadth, inconsistent quality — Daraz/Saino territory | Coherent, premium, controlled | Coherent front, flexible back |
| Differentiation | None — Saino, Swodeshi, Himali Green already exist [E] | Maximum — vacant position [E] | Same as B externally |
| Margins | 3%-commission economics (Himali Green caps at Rs 100/product [E]) | Product margin 50–65% typical for curated craft retail [SI] | Product margin |
| Scalability | High SKU scale, low value scale | Bounded by curation capacity | Expandable supply under quality gate |
| Brand value | Platform utility, low | High | High |
| Quality control | Structurally impossible (seller self-service) | Full | Full at the gate |
| Community relationships | Transactional onboarding | Deep, few | Deep core + widening ecosystem |
| Operational complexity | Two-sided: vendor tools, disputes, payouts — Saino needed a second app [E] | Lowest | Medium (intake pipeline) |

**Recommendation: Model B at launch, architected for Model C.**
Marketplace is triple-disqualified: the lane is occupied [E], the economics are commission-thin [E], and quality control — the one thing a premium provenance brand cannot compromise — is structurally impossible in seller-self-service [SI]. Timro and Local Project prove curation works in Nepal; The Citizenry proves it scales globally. "Architected for C" costs almost nothing now: makers/communities as first-class entities, per-maker attribution and earnings ledger — the same schema the USP already requires. If supply scales, MUD opens the intake gate wider without changing the customer-facing model. What it never builds: vendor self-service listing, seller apps, commission billing.

## 7. Implications for the Existing Design Direction

### What changes because of Nepal-specific competition
- Nothing in the premium-editorial direction *reverses* — the Nepal research strengthens it: the local visual field is split between NGO-institutional (Mahaguthi, WSDO) and tech-marketplace (Himali Green, Saino), so warm editorial minimalism has **zero local occupancy** [E].
- **Price display becomes a positioning act**: fixed, confident NPR pricing everywhere, prominently — against a field of quote-only and haggle-priced competitors, the visible price IS a differentiator [E→R].
- **Payment trust marks localize**: eSewa/Khalti/COD logos belong in the footer and at the payment step from day one — the premium cohort doesn't have them; the marketplace cohort does; MUD must have both premium feel and local rails [E→R].
- **Operational polish is a weapon, not hygiene**: competitors leak trust through 404s, boilerplate refund policies, sold-out grids, and dead shops [E]. Working links, a real returns policy in plain language, and honest stock states outperform any visual flourish locally.

### What becomes more prominent
- **Maker/community attribution on the PDP buy column** — the single clearest gap in the market [E].
- **Nepali craft vocabulary as product language**: dhakiya, feruwa, deluwa, sikki, kans, allo, lokta — names no competitor uses on-shelf, verified anthropological depth available (Knotcraft's typologies) [E→R]. Product naming pattern: *"Feruwa storage basket — sikki grass, Rautahat"*.
- **The wage/methodology page** — because Mahaguthi's 68% and Dinadi's disclosure set a high local evidence bar [E].
- **Gifting** (festival + corporate) as a merchandised path, given the Giftmandu gap [E].

### What gets removed
- Fair-trade badge-forward framing — that identity belongs to the 40-year cohort; MUD substantiates differently (methodology page, consent policy, named provenance) [R].
- Generic "empowering artisans" copy — table stakes, and banned vocabulary per the impact playbook [E→R].
- Any "treasure trove" abundance merchandising — Dhukuti owns abundance; MUD's small catalogue must read as edited, not thin [R].

### Signature interaction candidate
**The origin trace.** On every PDP, a compact provenance strip in the buy column: `Maker → Community, District → Material → Hours`. Tapping it expands into the full origin path (maker profile, community page, material page) without leaving the product. It is the USP made tangible, it is the thing no competitor can render (they lack the data), and it is cheap: one component over the provenance schema [R].

### MUD-specific IA pattern
**Craft vocabulary as first-class taxonomy**: `/craft/sikki-grass`, `/craft/allo`, basket-typology pages (`/craft/dhakiya`) — each shoppable, each feeding SEO where zero Nepali competitors publish structured craft content [E→R]. Combined with the bidirectional maker↔product rule from `10-information-architecture.md`, this is an information architecture no local player can mirror without MUD's data model.

### What to avoid visually (locally overused)
- Prayer-flag palette, Buddha/thangka/khukuri/singing-bowl iconography — souvenir-cohort signifiers [E].
- Felt-craft cuteness (red-cheeked dolls, felt balls) as brand imagery — Nepal's most crowded craft category [E].
- NGO sage-green + kraft + smiling-beneficiary photography — the institutional cohort's register [E].
- Marketplace UI tropes: badge sprays, seller ratings, "verified" ticks without methodology [E].
