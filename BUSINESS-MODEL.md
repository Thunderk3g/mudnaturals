# MUD Naturals — Business Model
### Purpose-driven concept store · Nepal · v1, August 2026

---

## 1. What MUD Naturals is

**MUD Naturals is a purpose-driven concept store bringing together natural products, traditional crafts, food and everyday goods created by independent Nepali makers and communities.** We discover, curate and develop these products for contemporary markets — creating dignified income for makers while preserving skills, knowledge and local materials.

**Philosophy:** People × Nature × Craft × Commerce.

**Positioning (from market research):** a commercial concept brand first, social enterprise second — never a charity shop. The promise customers see: **"objects with origins"** — every product shows who made it, where, and from what. The 80/20 model is the engine behind that promise, not the headline in front of it.

---

## 2. Mission · Vision · Core statement

**Mission.** To create a sustainable bridge between Nepali makers and the market, where traditional knowledge and natural materials become dignified livelihoods and beautiful, useful products.

**Vision.** Nepal's most trusted name for community-made natural living — the place where authentic craft, natural care and honest food meet contemporary life.

**Core statement (About page / investor one-pager):**
> MUD Naturals is a purpose-driven concept store that brings together natural products, traditional crafts, food and everyday goods created by independent makers and communities. We curate and develop these products for contemporary markets, creating dignified income opportunities while preserving skills, knowledge and local resources. Through our sustainable model, 80% of the value we generate strengthens and grows the enterprise, while 20% is dedicated to the MUD Impact Fund — turning every purchase into an opportunity for shared growth.

## 3. The 80/20 model — exact mechanics

### The rule
For every Rs. 1,000 of **net profit**:
- **Rs. 800 → MUD Naturals**: operations, salaries, product development, marketing, reinvestment, growth.
- **Rs. 200 → MUD Impact Fund**: artisan equipment, skills training, fair-price premiums, product-development grants, community projects.

### Measurement base — decide once, publish once
Recommended: **20% of audited net profit, with a floor.** Reasons:
- 20% of *revenue* would likely make a young retail business insolvent.
- 20% of *net profit alone* risks a Rs. 0 fund in loss-making early years — and publishing "Impact Fund: Rs 0" in year one destroys the credibility the model exists to create.

**Recommended formula (publish exactly this):**
> *The MUD Impact Fund receives 20% of annual net profit, or 1% of annual revenue — whichever is greater.*

The 1%-of-revenue floor guarantees the fund is never empty, costs a young business very little, and matches an established credibility signal (Drokpo, a local competitor, is a "1% for the Planet" member — the mechanism is already understood in this market).

### Critical framing rules (from competitive + impact research)
1. **Never say "80% for us, 20% for the community."** Say: *"80% sustains the enterprise. 20% fuels our impact."*
2. **Never lead with 80/20 in Nepal.** Competitor benchmark: Mahaguthi publicly claims *68% of sales revenue returns to artisans*. Against that, "20% of profit" invites a losing comparison. So the public impact story has **two layers, in this order**:
   - **Layer 1 — the trade itself (the big number):** what MUD pays makers. Fair, fixed prices agreed with makers, published as *"X% of each product's price goes to its maker"* per product or per category. This is where MUD competes with the 68% claim — trade, not aid.
   - **Layer 2 — the fund (the extra):** the 80/20 Impact Fund on top of fair trade, for equipment, training and development. This is what nobody else in the market formalises.
3. **Makers are partners and producers, never beneficiaries.** Banned copy: "help", "support", "empower", "give back", "underprivileged", "every purchase changes a life". Correct register: *"We work with independent makers and producers to bring their skills, stories and products to wider markets."*
4. **Publish limitations.** One honest paragraph on what the numbers do and don't cover. It is the cheapest credibility available and pre-empts every gotcha.

### Governance (minimum viable)
- Separate ledger account for the Fund from day one (not a separate legal entity yet).
- Fund disbursements decided with maker representatives in the room, twice a year.
- Every disbursement logged: date, amount, recipient community, purpose.
- Annual **MUD Impact Report** (template in §8).

## 4. The three pillars

**1 · DISCOVER — Curate.** Find authentic products from small producers, artisans and marginalized communities. Every product passes two bars: *contemporary usefulness* and *documentable origin*. If provenance can't be captured, it doesn't launch. Curation is the moat — MUD is edited, never an everything-catalogue.

**2 · DEVELOP — Add value.** MUD is not a reseller. Per product line we add: packaging, branding, quality improvement, product development, photography, storytelling, digital marketing, premium positioning, market access. This is the commercial justification for MUD's margin — makers' products become viable at prices they could never reach alone.

**3 · RETURN — Share the value.** Fair fixed prices at purchase (layer 1) + the Impact Fund (layer 2): tools and equipment, skills training, new-product development grants, education, capacity building, community-led initiatives.

**The ecosystem loop:**
```
COMMUNITIES & PRODUCERS  (artisans · farmers · small producers · traditional makers)
        ↓ fair fixed prices, agreed together
MUD NATURALS  (curate → improve → brand → package → market)
        ↓
CUSTOMERS  (beautiful, useful, authentic natural products)
        ↓
REVENUE → 80% enterprise growth · 20% MUD Impact Fund
        ↓
more capability, tools, training → back to communities
```

## 5. Product categories (concept-store scope)

| Category | Examples | Sourcing notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Craft & Home** | sikki/kans baskets (dhakiya, feruwa, deluwa), allo textiles, lokta paper goods, homeware | Core identity category; deepest provenance stories |
| **Natural Care** | organic oils, natural cosmetics, handmade soaps | Proven Nepali supply (Wild Earth-class producers, Himalayan Bio Trade-class community-forest oils); needs ingredient/batch labeling |
| **Food & Pantry** | teas, honeys, traditional candies, spices | Requires expiry/batch/food-labeling compliance (DFTQC); highest repeat-purchase rate — the retention engine |
| **Candles & Ritual** | natural/handmade candles, incense | High-margin, gift-friendly |
| **Gifting** | curated gift boxes mixing all categories | The revenue engine: corporate (Dashain/Tihar — banks/IT firms currently buy chocolate hampers; craft-grade gifting is an open lane) + diaspora send-home gifts |

Discipline rule: 4–5 categories, each edited hard. Food and cosmetics are the *frequency* categories (repeat purchases, lower price points Rs 300–1,500); craft is the *identity and margin* category (Rs 1,500–8,000); gifting is the *volume* category. This mix fixes curated-craft's classic weakness — low purchase frequency.

## 6. Customers & revenue streams

**Customers, in priority order:**
1. Urban Kathmandu/Lalitpur professionals, 25–45, buying for their own homes and gifting (proven band Rs 2,000–8,000; food/care entry at Rs 300–1,500).
2. Corporate buyers — festival and event gifting (recurring, forecastable volume).
3. Nepali diaspora — gifts delivered to family in Nepal (no export logistics needed at launch).
4. Phase 2: tourists/expats, export wholesale, international D2C.

**Revenue streams:** online D2C store (eSewa/Khalti/COD) · corporate & bulk gifting · physical presence later (pop-ups → concept store, the Timro model) · phase-2 export/wholesale.

## 7. Community-partner model

- **Partner agreement per community/maker**: fixed prices agreed together (not per-order haggling), payment terms (recommended: pay on delivery of stock, not consignment — makers get cash immediately), quality specs, lead times.
- **Consent record per maker**: written permission for name, photo, quote use — revocable, dated. Publish the consent policy: *no competitor does this; it is both ethics and a trust signal.*
- **Attribution**: community-level by default, named individuals where consented (the maker page pattern).
- **Development ladder**: new partner → first curated SKU → co-developed product line → Impact Fund eligibility (equipment/training grants).
